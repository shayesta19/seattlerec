## Three national parks

**Mount Rainier**, **Olympic**, and **North Cascades** are all within a few hours' drive. Olympic alone contains a temperate rainforest, an alpine ridge, and a wild Pacific coastline — three completely different landscapes in one park.

Hiking is not a hobby here so much as a default weekend. **Washington Trails Association** (wta.org) is the community-maintained source everyone actually uses for trip reports and conditions. Check it before you drive anywhere.

Most trailheads need a parking pass — a **Northwest Forest Pass**, a **Discover Pass**, or a national parks pass depending on whose land it is. Get this right or get a ticket.

## Yes, those are volcanoes

Mount Rainier, Mount Baker, Glacier Peak, Mount Adams, and Mount St. Helens are all **active volcanoes** in Washington. St. Helens erupted in 1980 and you can visit what is left.

Rainier is the one that matters: it is heavily glaciated, and the hazard is less the eruption than the **lahar** — a fast mudflow down the river valleys. Several towns south of Seattle have lahar sirens and evacuation routes posted. Seattle itself is not in the path.

## Water, if that is your thing

If you are coming from a city with a river you used to sit next to, here are the equivalents:

- **Lake Union** — the middle of the city, kayaks and paddleboards for rent, houseboats, the best skyline view from water level
- **Green Lake** — a flat three-mile loop that half the city walks
- **Lake Washington** — swimming beaches on the east side of the city, Madison Park and Seward Park
- **Alki Beach** — West Seattle, sand, a genuine sunset, and the Space Needle across the bay
- **Golden Gardens** — Ballard, driftwood fires, the local move on a summer evening
- **Discovery Park** — a bluff, a lighthouse, and enough trail to forget you are in a city

Summer here is a water city. Boat rentals, paddleboards, and lake swimming are the best use of July and August, and most visitors never think of it.

## The big one

Washington sits on the **Cascadia Subduction Zone**, which is capable of a magnitude 9 earthquake and a tsunami, and is overdue by geological standards. Locals live with this cheerfully. Worth knowing, not worth worrying about on a four-day trip.
