## Ditch the Starbucks

Yes, it started here. The Pike Place store is a photo, not a coffee. Seattle's actual coffee culture is in the **independent roasters and neighborhood cafes**, and the gap in quality is not subtle. Any given neighborhood has two or three places better than anything with a logo you recognize.

Cafes double as the city's living rooms — see the third places section — so picking a good one matters more here than in most cities.

## The food is Asian, and that is the point

Seattle's best eating runs through its **Chinese, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Filipino, Thai, and Indian** kitchens, and a lot of it is in unglamorous buildings on arterial roads. The Chinatown-International District, the stretch of Aurora, and the Eastside strip malls are all worth more of your time than most of downtown.

## Teriyaki is a Seattle story

You will hear "teriyaki was invented in Seattle." Not exactly — the technique is centuries old in Japan. What *was* invented here is **Seattle-style teriyaki**: charcoal-grilled chicken, a sweeter sauce, a scoop of white rice, a little cabbage salad, under ten dollars, in and out in fifteen minutes.

Toshihiro Kasahara opened **Toshi's Teriyaki on Roy Street in Lower Queen Anne on March 2, 1976**, and the format spread across the city and then the country. At its peak Seattle had hundreds of teriyaki shops. Many are family-run and quietly disappearing, which is a good reason to eat at one.

## Practical notes

- Kitchens close early. A 9pm dinner plan is a risk.
- Reservations matter more than the casual vibe suggests.
- Happy hour is a real institution here and is often the best value in an expensive city.
- Farmers markets are excellent and run year-round in several neighborhoods.
