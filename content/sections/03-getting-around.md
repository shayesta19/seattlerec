## Get an ORCA card

One card, every agency — buses, light rail, streetcar, water taxi, ferries, the works. You can buy a physical card or add ORCA to your phone's wallet. Do this before you do anything else.

The **Transit GO Ticket** app is the alternative if you want mobile tickets without the card.

## Buses do most of the work

This is a bus city first. The network is dense, the routes are frequent on the main corridors, and OneBusAway or Google Maps will both get you there. Real-time arrival information is good.

**Check for reroutes before you leave.** There is always construction somewhere, and it moves stops without much warning.

## Light rail

The **1 Line** runs north–south through the city — Lynnwood down through the University District, downtown, the stadiums, the airport, and on south. The airport connection is the single best reason to know it exists: it goes straight downtown for the price of a bus ride.

The **2 Line** serves the Eastside — Bellevue and Redmond — and as of March 2026 it crosses Lake Washington on the I-90 bridge, connecting the Eastside directly to downtown for the first time.

There is also a **T Line** in Tacoma, and two short streetcar lines in Seattle that are more novelty than transport.

## Water taxi and ferries

The **King County Water Taxi** runs from downtown to West Seattle and Vashon. Fast, cheap, and a genuinely beautiful commute.

**Washington State Ferries** for Bainbridge, Bremerton, and the rest.

## Bikes are the best-kept secret

This is the part most visitors miss. Seattle is far more bikeable than its hills suggest, because the good routes follow old rail grades and waterfronts and stay flat:

- The **Burke-Gilman Trail** runs about 20 miles from Ballard through the university and around the north end of Lake Washington
- The **Elliott Bay Trail** along the downtown waterfront
- The **I-90 Trail** across Lake Washington to Mercer Island and Bellevue — a bike path on a floating bridge, which is exactly as good as it sounds
- The **Alki Trail** in West Seattle, flat and right on the beach

**Lime** and other rentals are everywhere, and e-bikes make the hills a non-issue.

## Driving

Possible, expensive, and mostly unnecessary in the city proper. Parking downtown and on Capitol Hill is genuinely painful. Rent a car for the day you go to the mountains, not for the week.
