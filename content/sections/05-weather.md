## The myth

Seattle averages about **38 inches of rain a year.** New York gets about 43. Boston gets about 44. Seattle does not crack the top 40 U.S. cities for annual rainfall.

## The reality

It is grey about **200 days a year.** The rain here is not storms — it is a fine, persistent drizzle spread across many months, the kind that never justifies an umbrella and soaks you anyway. Locals genuinely do not use umbrellas. A hood is the local technology.

## The year, month by month

**Late June through September is the payoff.** Long dry stretches, temperatures in the seventies, sunlight until after 9pm because Seattle is farther north than Toronto. This is one of the best summers of any city in America and it is the reason people put up with the rest.

**October through May is the grey.** Not cold — it rarely freezes and it rarely snows more than a few times a year — just dim. Sunset in December is around 4:20pm.

**"June gloom"** is a local term for the way early summer can stay overcast until the switch flips, usually right around the Fourth of July.

## What to actually pack

A rain shell with a hood, layers, and shoes you do not mind getting wet. Leave the umbrella. Bring sunglasses anyway — low winter sun through wet streets is blinding.
