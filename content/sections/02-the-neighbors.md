People say "Seattle" and mean a metro area of about four million people spread across water and hills. The other names you will hear:

**Bellevue** — directly east across Lake Washington, about 20 minutes without traffic. Glossier, newer, more corporate, better Chinese food than most of Seattle. Now connected by light rail.

**Redmond** — further east, past Bellevue. Microsoft's home. Suburban, green, ends of light rail lines.

**Kirkland** — northeast, on the lake's eastern shore. A walkable downtown with a waterfront, a good summer destination.

**Bainbridge Island** — 35 minutes west by ferry from downtown. The classic first-time-visitor move: ride over, walk the town, ride back at sunset. The ferry ride is the attraction as much as the island.

**Vashon Island** — south, reached by a different ferry. Rural, artsy, deliberately unpolished.

**The rest of the islands** — Whidbey, Camano, Orcas, San Juan, Lopez. The San Juans are a serious trip north, not a day trip, and worth it in summer.

**Tacoma** to the south, **Everett** to the north, both real cities in their own right.

**Portland** is under three hours by car and a different state.

## Ferries are transit, not tourism

Washington State Ferries is the largest ferry system in the United States and locals use it to commute. Walking on is cheap and needs no reservation. Driving on in summer means a wait, sometimes a long one, and some routes take reservations well in advance. Walk on unless you have a reason not to.
