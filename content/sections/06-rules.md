## It closes early

Seattle is a **morning city**. Kitchens close earlier than you expect, and plenty of local businesses shut at 2, 3, or 4pm — especially cafes and bakeries. Very few places are open past midnight.

Hours are also **genuinely unpredictable**: different on weekdays than weekends, different in summer than winter, and small places change them without updating anything. Check before you go, and check the actual business's page, not just the search result.

## There is no club scene

Say this plainly: if you are coming here expecting a proper nightclub city, **you will be disappointed.** That is not what Seattle is.

What Seattle has instead is a good **dive bar and speakeasy** scene — small rooms, cocktails made by people who care, no line, no dress code. Lean into that and you will have a great night. Fight it and you will spend an expensive evening being underwhelmed.

Also worth knowing: **drinks at clubs, concert venues, and stadiums are weak and expensive.** Real bars pour honestly. Venues do not.

## Nobody dresses up

Black, grey, navy, dark green, and a technical jacket. That is the palette, year round, and it is barely an exaggeration. Fleece is formalwear.

If you dress nicely you will not be judged, exactly — you will be **noticed**, which is worse. Dressing down here is not laziness; it is the local code for not making it a thing.

## The tax

**About 10.55% sales tax** in Seattle, one of the highest combined rates in the country, and it is not included in the price you see on the shelf. Budget for it.

There is also a **sweetened beverage tax** of 1.75 cents per ounce, which is why a bottle of soda costs noticeably more here than across the city line.

Eating out is expensive. Tipping expectations are high. A casual dinner for two adds up faster than you expect.
