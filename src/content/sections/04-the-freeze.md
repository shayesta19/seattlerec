Here is the thing everyone warns you about, and they are not wrong.

**The Freeze is not unfriendliness.** People will hold doors, give directions, apologize for things that are not their fault, and chat pleasantly with you in a line. What they will not do is turn that into a plan. "We should get coffee sometime" is a complete sentence here, not an opening move. Nobody is lying to you — they mean it warmly and they will not follow up.

## Why it is real

A few things stack:

- **Almost nobody is from here.** A huge share of the city moved in for a job. Transplant cities are friendlier on the surface and harder to root in.
- **Social life is organized around activities, not hanging out.** People have a climbing gym, a run club, a hiking group, a board game night. The friendship happens inside the activity.
- **Nine months of dark.** From October to May, the default answer to an evening invitation is home.
- **Tech work culture** rewards scheduling over spontaneity. Calendars are full and booked out weeks.

## How to beat it

The move is **recurrence, not intensity.** One great conversation goes nowhere. Showing up to the same thing every Tuesday for two months turns strangers into friends almost automatically.

- Join a thing with a fixed schedule — run club, climbing gym, bouldering, dodgeball, trivia night, a choir, a volunteer shift
- Say yes to the group hike, not the coffee
- Be the one who sends the calendar invite; people here will happily accept a specific plan and will rarely make one
- Give it two winters before you judge

Newcomers who leave usually leave in year two. The ones who stay found their Tuesday thing.
